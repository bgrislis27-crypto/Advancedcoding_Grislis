The Backrooms

A first-person maze. The halls are yellow, the lights fail on their own, and the picture can go black. Find the green exit before three minutes run out.

Play the game View the code

After you wake up

Yellow paper, a tiled ceiling, and a clock

Each visit builds a new maze. The halls turn, meet, and end, and they look alike on purpose. There is no map. The wallpaper repeats, the carpet is speckled, and the fluorescent panels stay on until a flash of black.

The blue clock under the arc counts down from 3:00. Reach the green exit before it hits zero. Run if you need to, try a door with E, and keep the flashlight charged.

From the menu to the exit

How a visit goes

The page opens on a still yellow hall. The title sits over it, and the lined buttons are the only things you click. Headphones help, because the buzz and the footsteps are part of finding your way.

  1. Choose a visit. More Options opens two extra columns. One is the game mode. One is the difficulty. The small line under the main menu shows the pair you picked, such as Classic · Normal.
  2. Wake up. Wake Up builds a new maze for that visit. Click the picture so the mouse can look around, then walk with W, A, S, and D.
  3. Keep moving. The halls repeat on purpose, and there is no map. A sign can name the kind of place you are in. Dead ends are dark enough to hide in. Offices feel still. Wet floors slow your steps.
  4. Leave, or run out of time. The green door is the exit. Stand at it and press E. The win screen says how much of the three minutes you had left. If the blue clock reaches 0:00 first, the visit ends with “The clock ran out.”

Shift spends stamina, so a sprint is a short burst and then a walk. F turns the flashlight on and off. The gold bar is the battery. It drains while the light is on, and it comes back while the light is off. A closed door in front of you asks for E. Some of those doors open. Some stay shut.

Before Wake Up

Modes and difficulty

Mode and difficulty are separate. You can play Deaf on Easy, or Free on Hard. The clock is three minutes in every combination. What changes is the sound, how often the picture goes black, and how hard it is to keep running and to keep the flashlight alive.

Classic

The usual visit. You hear the halls, and the lights cut out at random.

Deaf

The same halls with no sound. The lights still cut out. You navigate by sight alone.

Free

Fewer flashes of darkness. The clock still runs, so the exit is still a race.

Easy

The darkness comes less often. Stamina lasts longer, and the flashlight drains more slowly. Standing in the dark unsettles you less.

Normal

A fair pace. Three minutes, a normal sprint, and a battery that needs watching.

Hard

The darkness comes more often. Sprinting empties the pale bar faster, and the gold battery falls faster too.

Fear never has its own bar. In the dark the fog thickens a little and the edges of the picture close in. A still office lets that settle. Nothing in the halls is chasing you. The pressure is the clock, the blackouts, and the sameness of the rooms.

One maze, several kinds of room

What the halls are made of

Each Wake Up runs a maze generator. It carves halls, opens a few rooms, and adds loops so you can circle back. The start is near one corner. The exit is a green door in a cell far from that start. You will not get the same layout twice.

The floor is divided into bands, so a long walk changes the kind of place even when the wallpaper stays yellow.

  • Yellow halls are the first stretch, close to where you wake up.
  • Offices are quieter. Fear falls faster there, and the flashlight recovers a little faster if you leave it off.
  • Wet floors drag on your walk. The same key press covers less ground.
  • Maintenance sits along the far side. Stairs and empty rooms show up late in the maze. The last band is stranger, and the signs stop being helpful.

Doors are placed on some of the walls between cells. A normal door slides open. A locked door tells you it is locked and stays in the way. An unexpected door drops you into a different kind of room. A vanishing door is there while you look at it, and gone if you turn away. Locked doors are kept off the start column and off the cell just before the exit, so a visit cannot be sealed shut.

Hiding spots are dead ends that are not the start and not the exit. Press E there and the camera crouches. Press E again to stand up. The prompt at the bottom of the screen is the only hint: try the door, hide, step out, or use the exit.

Controls

Mouse
Look around. Click the screen if the mouse is not locked.
W A S D
Walk.
Shift
Run. The stamina bar drops while you run and fills when you stop. Steps get louder.
F
Flashlight. The gold bar is the battery. It flickers when the battery is low.
E
Try a door, hide in a dark corner, step back out, or use the exit.
Arrow keys
Turn left and right if the mouse is not locked.

What is in the game

  • Yellow wallpaper, carpet, and ceiling lights.
  • Lights that flicker alone. One can stay off for a few seconds.
  • Fog that makes distant halls darker than the space around you.
  • Connected areas: yellow halls, offices, wet floors, maintenance, stairs, empty rooms, and stranger halls.
  • Doors. Some open, some are locked, some lead to another kind of room, and a few vanish if you look away.
  • A flashlight. Fear is hidden: the picture gets a little worse in the dark, and a still room lets it settle.
  • Random flashes of darkness. There is no monster in the halls.
  • Footsteps, a light buzz, sounds from different directions, and stretches of silence.
  • A three-minute clock. You win at the exit. You lose if the time runs out.
